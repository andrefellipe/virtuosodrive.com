### What I checked and found sound

- **Traceability.** Every requirement ID in `traceability.json` exists in the documents. NFR1.20, NFR2.20 to NFR2.25, NFR3.20 to NFR3.27, NFR5.20 to NFR5.26, NFR6.20 to NFR6.26, NFR7.20 to NFR7.22, NFR8.20 and NFR8.21, NFR9.20 to NFR9.27, NFR10.20 to NFR10.36, NFR11.20 to NFR11.25, NFR12.20 to NFR12.24, NFR13.20, NFR14.20, NFR15.20 and NFR15.21 all resolve. There are no duplicate IDs within U3, and the IDs are carried correctly by the "From" columns.
- **Cost arithmetic.** 30 readers every 3 seconds is 10 requests a second and about 26 million requests in a 30-day month. At about US$1 per million requests for the entry point that is about US$26, as stated. The warning's arithmetic is right, but see the finding on what it leaves out.
- **Fit with the design.** The security rules match the design on read-only operations, identical not-found answers, the exact field set (BR1.7), and the 64-level walk. The reliability rules follow BR2.9 to BR2.11 (all-or-nothing change, one at a time per folder). The target for the number of statements (NFR9.22) is reachable with one recursive query for the path, and the row lock (T28) does not block readers.
- **No new library** (T21 to T23, T31): reasonable to decide without a question, in line with the rule that new libraries need approval. No new fixed-cost AWS resource is added apart from the metric series noted above.
- **Time budget.** About 0.3 seconds for the journey, 0.5 seconds at the server and the rest for 30 KB or so of compressed answer and rendering is plausible. A fresh connection from Brazil costs more round trips than the 0.3 seconds assumes, but there is room.

### What I could not check

- U1's quality requirements (`construction/u1-thin-slice/nfr-requirements/`) could not be opened in this review: the review's read scope refused access to another Unit's files. I therefore could not verify whether U3 contradicts U1's access-password requirement, U1's own numbering (beyond the NFR15.10 reference), the US$60 ceiling, the Lambda timeout and memory settings, or whether the API is behind the page's distribution. Nothing in U3's documents contradicts U1's password on its face (reads need no account; the access password is described as the protection while environments are private), but that is unconfirmed.
- Whether PostgreSQL 18 on Amazon RDS has the normal-form and case-folding functions that T26 relies on. The document leaves it unverified.
- Real timings, and the actual size of the 500-item sample.

### Summary

The requirements are thorough and mostly testable, and the references resolve. NOT-READY rests on three points that a developer would otherwise have to decide alone. The 20,000-item claim and the answer-size figures contradict each other. The "unchanged" answer is not required to respect a deleted ancestor. The name-comparison check has no expected results while T26 lets a fallback weaken the design's case rule. The remaining items are smaller fixes to checks and to how later Units are bound.
